## Common Femoral Artery Endarterectomy with Iliac Intervention

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) Peripheral Artery Disease
2) BLE Tissue loss

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) Left CFA endarterectomy and patch angioplasty repair
2) Ultrasound guided right common femoral artery access
3) Aorto-iliac angiogram
4) Kissing iliac stent placement (8mmx29mm VBX on R CIA and 9mmx29mm VBX on L)
5) Completion aorto-iliac angiogram
6) L Above Knee Amputation

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** Sean Zarrabi, PA
**ANESTHESIA:** General

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 50 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** Left AKA
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** 150ml

**FINDINGS:**
Palpable femoral pulses at the end of the case.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of general anesthesia, the patient's groins were prepped and draped, along with the left lower extremity, in the usual fashion. We started with a longitudinal incision over the common femoral artery with a 10 blade, followed by electrocautery dissection down through the subcutaneous tissues and through the femoral fascia, exposing the common femoral artery and the superficial femoral artery. We then used Metzenbaum scissors for mobilization, dissection, and control of the superficial femoral artery, the profunda, and the proximal common femoral artery, along with all the branches. The larger vessels were controlled with vessel loops while the smaller branches were controlled with 2-0 Silk sutures. Next, we administered 7,000 units of heparin and accessed the common femoral artery directly with a micropuncture needle, followed by advancement of a micropuncture wire under fluoroscopic guidance. We then exchanged it for a stiff wire and upsized to a 7 French sheath. Next, we used ultrasound guidance to access the right common femoral artery with a micropuncture needle. We subsequently exchanged for a stiff wire and placed a 7 French sheath.

Next, we selected for the abdominal aorta from both femoral arteries and performed an aortogram through a short catheter, revealing a patent abdominal aorta with high-grade stenosis of over 90% of the right common femoral artery and about 60% stenosis of the left common femoral artery. These lesions were amenable to treatment with kissing iliac stents. We placed our Storq wires in position and deployed an 8 millimeter by 29 millimeter VBX viabahn stent graft on the right and a 7 millimeter by 29 millimeter VBX viabahn stent graft on the left. We then closed the right CFA with Perclose device, holding manual compression for 5 minutes.

Next we clamped the left common femoral and superficial femoral arteries as well as the profunda femoris artery then made a longitudinal arteriotomy using an 11 blade, exposing the underlying occlusive plaque. We performed a common femoral endarterectomy extending into the superficial femoral artery with a Freer elevator device. After completing the endarterectomy, we tacked down the intima within the superficial femoral artery with interrupted 7-0 sutures. Next, we performed a patch repair with bovine pericardium using running 6-0 Prolene sutures. Prior to completing the repair, we flushed antegrade and retrograde, then completed the anastomosis. Hemostasis was achieved with reversal using 30 mg of protamine, as well as several 6-0 Prolene repair sutures and additional Surgicel to assist in hemostasis. We then irrigated the wound copiously and closed it in three layers, starting with a 2-0 Vicryl layer, followed by a 3-0 layer, and finally a 4-0 Monocryl layer in the dermis. We dressed the wound on the left as well as the puncture site on the right and then turned our attention to the LLE for AKA.

We fashioned a fish mouth incision in the mid-portion of the left thigh, then used a scalpel to make an incision followed by a lateral cut to dissect down through the subcutaneous tissues and fascia, utilizing electrocautery as well to dissect through the muscle. We identified the femoral vein and artery, which were clamped and then tied with 2-0 silk sutures as well as 3-0 Prolene sutures. The periosteum of the femoral bone was exposed and we elevated the periosteum before transecting the bone with an oscillating saw. We irrigated the wound, and once we were satisfied with hemostasis, we closed the fascia with interrupted 2-0 Vicryl sutures, followed by stapling of the skin. The wound was dressed with a Xeroform, Kerlex gauze and a coban dressing.

The patient tolerated the procedure well, was extubated, and brought to the recovery room with stable vital signs.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

## Lower Extremity Angioplasty / Stenting

### Sample Report 1 - SFA Recanalization

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) RLE rest pain
2) Peripheral artery disease

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) Ultrasound guided access of the left common femoral artery
2) Left fem-check angiogram
3) Selection for the right SFA
4) RLE angiogram
5) Crossing of the R SFA
6) Balloon angioplasty of the R SFA with 4mmx 150mm balloon
7) Stenting of the R SFA with a 6mmx 150mm Eluvia stent distally and 6mm x 40mm Eluvia stent overlapping proximally
8) Post-PTA of the R SFA with a 5mm x 150mm balloon
9) Completion angiogram
10) Closure of the L CFA with a closure device

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local anesthesia with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 10 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** No foley

**FINDINGS:**
R mid SFA occlusion which was able to be recanalized with a stent

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the left groin was prepped and draped in the usual fashion ultrasound was used to evaluate the left common femoral artery. The left common femoral artery was accessed using ultrasound guidance with a micropuncture needle. A micropuncture wire was advanced and a micropuncture sheath was placed. A fem-check angiogram showed our puncture to be in the mid common femoral artery.

Next we selected for the left superficial femoral artery using a crossover catheter. We performed a RLE angiogram which showed a patent left CFA, SFA proximally with a mid-SFA occlusion and reconstitution of the distal SFA and popliteal artery. The patient was noted to have three vessel runoff otherwise.

We then upsized to a long 6Fr Destination sheath and administered 8,000 units of heparin. We then crossed the SFA occlusion with an angled glide wire through a quick cross catheter. We confirmed that we were in the popliteal artery with our catheter via an angiogram then placed a Stork wire and performed our first balloon angioplasty with a 4mmx150mm Mustang Balloon. Next we placed a 6mmx150mm Eluvia self expanding stent and a 6mm x 40mm Eluvia stent proximally. We then post angioplastied this with a 5mmx150mm Mustang balloon.

A completion angiogram showed a patent SFA and three vessel runoff. We closed the artery with a Perclose closure device and the patient was brought to the recovery room with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

## Thrombectomy / Embolectomy

### Sample Report 1 - Thrombolysis and Suction Thrombectomy

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) RLE Acute Limb Ischemia
2) On-going RLE thrombolysis (EKOS catheter)

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) RLE angiogram
2) Removal of EKOS thrombolysis catheter
3) RLE suction thrombectomy (Penumbra Cat6)
4) Stenting of R distal popliteal artery (6mmx40mm Self Expanding Balloon)
5) PTA of distal popliteal artery with a 5mm balloon
6) Closure of L CFA access with a Perclose device
7) RLE 4 compartment fasciotomy

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** LMA

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 50 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** 200 ml

**FINDINGS:**
RLE popliteal artery high grade stenosis (distal to stent which had been placed), SFA otherwise patent, Some thrombus in the proximal popliteal stent which was removed with suction thrombectomy. Three vessel runoff to ankle.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the left groin and RLE was prepped and draped in the usual fashion we removed the EKOS catheter over a Storq wire. We then performed a RLE angiogram through the indwelling 6Fr sheath.

We noted the SFA to be patent however the popliteal stent which had been placed was patent proximally with a high grade lesion at the distal end. This was crossed with an angled Storq Wire. Thrombus was also noted in the popliteal stent. We performed suction thrombectomy using a Penumbra Cat 6 catheter. This removed the clot on follow up angiograms. Next we stented the distal popliteal artery narrowing with a 6mmx40mm Stent and performed balloon angioplasty using a 5mmx40mm balloon. Completion angiogram showed resolution of the stenosis with 3 vessel runoff to the ankle.

Next the sheath was pulled back and the access site closed using a Perclose device. 1% lidocaine plain had been used to infiltrate around the indwelling sheath. The site was dressed with a D-stat type dressing.

Next we turned our attention to the RLE and a 4 compartment fasciotomy was performed by first making a longitudinal incision over the medial and lateral aspect of the right calf. Laterally we cauterized the transversely then longitudinally both above and below the septum to open up both the anterior and the posterior compartments. Medially we transected longitudinally and reflected and took down the soleus to expose the deep compartment. After obtaining hemostasis we dressed the wounds with dry gauze, kerlex and an ace bandage.

The patient was extubated and brought to the recovery room and then ICU with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery and then ICU in stable condition

### Sample Report 3 - LLE Suction Thrombectomy

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) Acute LLE ischemia
2) LLE thromboembolism

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) Ultrasound guided access of the right common femoral artery
2) Right fem-check angiogram
3) Selection for the left femoral artery
4) LLE angiogram
5) Selection of both the left tibioperoneal trunk artery
6) Suction thrombectomy of L tibioperoneal trunk and L AT, PT and peroneal arteries with Penumbra Bolt 6 suction thrombectomy catheter + Penumbra Bolt 7 suction catheter
7) PTA of tibioperoneal trunk and peroneal artery with a 3mm x 200mm balloon
8) Completion LLE angiogram
9) Open thromboembolectomy of left Posterior Tibial and Anterior Tibial Arteries
10) Closure of artery with Perclose device

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local anesthesia with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 15 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** No foley
**TRANSFUSION:** 2 Units PRBC

**FINDINGS:**
Thromboembolism in the left tibioperoneal trunk of likely organized fibrotic tissue. We could not get much thrombus using the penumbra device. We did get some thrombus from the tibial arteries however were never able to establish pulsatile flow. Due to the patient's frail status and instability even with conscious sedation the decision was made NOT to offer or perform general anesthesia to perform an open popliteal thromboembolectomy. It is important to note that the patient did not have sensation or movement of the foot at the beginning of the case, thus had a non viable foot from the start and exposing her to the risk of general anesthesia or more surgery was not warranted.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the right groin was prepped and draped in the usual fashion ultrasound was used to evaluate the right common femoral artery. The right common femoral artery was accessed using ultrasound guidance with a micropuncture needle. A micropuncture wire was advanced and a micropuncture sheath was placed. A fem-check angiogram showed our puncture to be in the mid common femoral artery.

Next we selected for the left superficial femoral artery using a crossover catheter. We performed a LLE angiogram which showed a patent left CFA, SFA and popliteal artery with a patent proximal AT with occlusion of the tibioperoneal trunk and occlusion of the proximal peroneal and PT arteries.

5,000 units of heparin were infused.

We first performed suction thrombectomy of the L tibioperoneal trunk and L AT, PT and peroneal arteries with Penumbra Bolt 6 suction thrombectomy catheter then a Penumbra Bolt 7 suction catheter. We only were able to pull back some thrombus with the Bolt 6 on the initial passage.

A repeat angiogram showed residual thrombus in the tibioperoneal trunk and for this reason we balloon angioplastied the tibioperoneal trunk and the peroneal artery with a 3mm x 200 mm balloon. This also did not improve the flow significantly. We did infuse 4mg of TPA through our thrombectomy catheter and flushed the catheter.

After poor success with the Penumbra device we moved to perform an open thromboembolectomy of the PT and the left AT arteries. These were exposed with longitudinal incisions at the level of the ankle after infiltration of lidocaine in the skin. We exposed the arteries and gained control proximally and distally before making a transverse arteriotomy and balloon thrombectomy with a 2 and 3 Fogarty balloon. We were able to pull out thrombus from the PT and were not able to pass the balloon proximally in the AT. We were never able to establish pulsatile flow, we did improve sluggish antegrade flow however.

These incisions were closed with interrupted 3-0 Nylon sutures and dressed. The groin puncture site was closed with a Perclose device. The heparin drip was restarted and the patient was brought to the ICU with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to ICU in stable condition

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of sedation, the patient's right upper extremity was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. A tourniquet was placed around the proximal upper arm on the right.
