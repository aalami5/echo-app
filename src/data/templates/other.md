## Below-Knee Amputation (BKA)

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) Non-healing R foot wound
2) R foot osteomyelitis

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
R LE Below-Knee Amputation

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** Sean Zarrabi
**ANESTHESIA:** General (LMA) + block

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 25 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** R below knee amputation specimen
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
Skin edges re-approximated well. Minimal tension.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of general anesthesia, the patient's right lower extremity was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. We marked out a standard classic posterior flap below the knee amputation. Then, Esmarch was used to extrude the venous blood from the right lower extremity. We turned up the tourniquet to 270 mmHg. Next, we used 10 blade to incise the skin around the marked area. We then utilized electrocautery to dissect down through the dermis up to the level of the fascia and through the fascia, exposing the underlying muscle medially, laterally, and posteriorly. Anteriorly we dissected down to the tibia.

The saphenous vein was identified and doubly ligated with 3-0 silk sutures. We then isolated the anterior tibial artery laterally after transecting through the anterior compartment muscles and the lateral compartment muscles. We used a right-angle clamp to encircle both the anterior tibial artery and vein, along with the deep peroneal nerve. This was doubly ligated with 3-0 silk sutures and transected. The interosseus membrane was cut with electrocautery. We were then able to circumferentially isolate the tibial bone at this level, as well as the fibula at this level. We raised the periosteum circumferentially around the fibula and the tibia and then used an oscillating handheld saw to transect through the tibia first just above the skin incision site. We did the same for the fibula, cutting it slightly shorter. The end of the tibia was beveled anteriorly. We then rounded the edges with a filing maneuver using our handheld oscillating saw. We next used the amputation knife to separate the tibia and fibula distally, leaving behind the gastrocnemius muscles for padding. Again, the peroneal and posterior tibial arteries were identified, ligated, and transected.

The anterior tibial artery was identified and ligated; we also identified the posterior tibial and peroneal arteries, which included the vein in a bundle. These were clamped with a right-angle clamp and tied first with 5-0 Prolene sutures circumferentially, followed by 3-0 silk sutures. We let down the tourniquet after about 33 minutes. All small areas of bleeding were either treated with electrocautery or with interrupted 3-0 silk suture ties. We used bone wax on the tibia and then re-approximated the fascial edges with interrupted 2-0 sutures. We placed as many sutures as were necessary such that we could not pass a finger through any of the gaps. Finally, we closed the skin with staples and dressed the wound.

The patient was extubated and brought to the recovery room with stable vital signs.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

## Central Line Placement

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Diverticulitis requiring long term antibiotics

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) Ultrasound guided Left internal jugular vein access
2) Placement of a Left internal jugular Single lumen Groshong catheter

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local anesthesia with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 5 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
The single port withdrew blood easily. Tip of the catheter was in the SVC.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the left neck was prepped and draped in the usual fashion ultrasound evaluation of the L internal jugular vein showed the vein to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos. 1% lidocaine plain was infiltrated at the planned site of vein access and a micropuncture needle was used to access the left internal jugular vein using ultrasound guidance. The micro-puncture wire was advanced into the jugular vein and a nick was made in the skin with an 11 blade at the wire entrance site. The micropuncture wire and inner dilator were removed and a short stiff Amplatz wire was advanced into the SVC using fluoroscopic guidance. Next the included two dilators were used to dilate the tract into the internal jugular vein using fluoroscopic guidance up to the peel-away sheath. The inner dilator and wire were removed and the tip of the tunneled catheter was advanced into the SVC. The Groshong catheter was then advanced into the peel away sheath until the tip was in the SVC.

Next we infiltrated the tunneling tract from two fingerbreadths below the mid-clavicular line to the neck wire access site in a curvilinear fashion.

A nick was made at the planned catheter exit site below the mid-clavicular line and the tract was dilated with a mosquito clamp. Next we tunneled the catheter with the included tunneling device from the neck incision to the chest incision.

The back end of the catheter was attached to the port after being trimmed and the sheath was placed over the junction. The port withdrew blood easily and was flushed first with normal saline followed by 1,000 units/ml of Heparin. A biopatch was placed around the catheter exit site and the catheter was secured with two interrupted 3-0 nylon sutures. The neck incision was closed with two interrupted 3-0 monocryl sutures and the sites were dressed with gauze and tegaderm.

The patient tolerated the procedure well and returned to the recovery room with vital signs stable.

This catheter is ok to be used for access.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

[Muscle viability at transection level, bleeding quality, tissue condition]

**DISPOSITION:**
[e.g., Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition, PM&R consulted for rehabilitation]
