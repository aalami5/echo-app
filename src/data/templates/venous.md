## Varicose Vein Treatment - GSV Closure + Microphlebectomies

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Symptomatic LLE GSV Reflux

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) ultrasound guided left distal calf great saphenous vein access
2) closure of left GSV from saphenofemoral junction to distal calves with Venaseal glue
3) Microphlebectomy of L calf incompetent varicosities / tributary veins x 15

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:**
**ANESTHESIA:** MAC + local

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** Minimal
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** vein segments which were discarded
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None

**FINDINGS:**
L saphenofemoral junction was noted to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of managed anesthesia care, the left lower extremity was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. We evaluated the left great saphenous veins from the saphenofemoral junction all the way down to the ankle and noted that the vein to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echoes. The great saphenous vein was noted to be quite prominent.

We then accessed the great saphenous veins just below the knee, using ultrasound guidance. We advanced the access needle with a micropuncture system and then advanced the micropuncture wire under ultrasound guidance, followed by placing our 7 French sheath. We then advanced our included venous wire, which was a 0.035 wire, all the way past the saphenofemoral junction and placed our guide catheters, all under ultrasound guidance. We then advanced our primed venous infusion catheter, attached them to the infusion guns, and deployed the Venaseal venous glue starting from 5 cm below the saphenofemoral junction all the way to the sheath exit site, while ensuring we were still in the vein using ultrasound guidance. We used the standard technique for the Venaseal for infusion of the string of beads of glue.

Next we infiltrated the previously marked sites where the patient had large incompetent varicosities / tributaries. We then used an 11 blade to make a nick in the skin the a vein hook to pull the tributary out and mosquito clamps to mobilize the vein segment before tying off each end with 3-0 silk sutures and resecting the vein segment which had been pulled out. Each incision was closed with a 5-0 monocryl suture and we placed steri-strips on the skin.

After completion, we confirmed that the common femoral vein and the saphenofemoral junction was fully compressible with no intrinsic echoes. We dressed the foot/calf and thigh with Kerlex gauze, followed by an Ace bandage.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

## Pulmonary Embolism Thrombectomy

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Saddle Pulmonary Embolism

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) ultrasound guided access of the right common femoral vein
2) femoral vein angiogram
3) Pre-close Perclose placement
3) selection of the pulmonary artery
4) Mechanical Suction Thrombectomy of the pulmonary artery with FlowTriever system
5) Pulmonary angiogram
6) Closure of the L CFA with a closure device

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:**
**ANESTHESIA:** local with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 30 mL
**FLUIDS:** no foley
**SPECIMENS:** thrombus. Discarded
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None

**FINDINGS:**
Excellent flow in pulmonary arteries post thrombectomy.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of sedation the patient's right groin was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. Ultrasound was used to evaluate the right common femoral artery which was noted to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos.

The right common femoral artery was accessed using ultrasound guidance with a micropuncture needle after infiltration of the planned site of access with 1% lidocaine plain. A micropuncture wire was advanced and a micropuncture sheath was placed. A fem-check angiogram showed our puncture to be in the mid common femoral vein with a patent external and common iliac vein.

Next we exchanged for a Storq wire and up sized to a 6Fr sheath. Next we used a Bernstein catheter to select for the superior vena cava and exchanged for a long Amplatz wire. We then placed the Intri 24 Sheath. Next we used an MIV catheter to select through the R atrium and ventricle into the pulmonary artery and performed our first pulmonary angiogram which showed a large saddle embolus. We then selected for the R inferior lobe with a Storq wire and a Bernstein catheter and and advanced our FlowTriever and performed the suction/mechanical thrombectomy procedure retrieving significant amount of thrombus. This was continued until we did not pull back any more thrombus. A follow up pulmonary angiogram showed much improved flow in the pulmonary arteries.

We removed our wires and sheaths and closed the femoral vein with the previously placed perclose device and also used the inari FlowStasis device.

Manual compression was held for 10 minutes.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

## PICC Line Placement

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) CNS Lymphoma with need for Chemo
2) need for IV access for chemo

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) ultrasound guided L brachial vein
2) selection for the SVC
3) placement of a dual lumen PICC (Bard Power Line)

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:**
**ANESTHESIA:** local + sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** Minimal
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of sedation the patient's LUE was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. The LUE mid upper arm brachial vein was identified with ultrasound and noted to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos. 1% lidocaine was used to infiltrate the planned site of access and a micropuncture needle was used to access the brachial vein. Next we advanced a glide wire selecting for the superior vena cava through a glide catheter. Next we trimmed the Bard Power Line catheter so the tip wound end up in the SVC and removed the sheath and placed our peel away sheath and advanced the catheter over the wire. ## IVC Filter Placement

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
1) Acute DVT
2) PE
3) Contraindication for anticoagulation

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) Ultrasound guided R common femoral vein access
2) IVC venogram
3) Placement of Denali Bard IVC filter

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local anesthesia with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 5 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
The CFV and IVC were patent with no thrombus. The Renal vein was patent.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the right groin was prepped and draped in the usual fashion ultrasound evaluation of the R common femoral vein showed the vein to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos. 1% lidocaine plain was infiltrated at the planned site of vein access and a micropuncture needle was used to access the right common femoral vein using ultrasound guidance. The micro-puncture wire was advanced into the common femoral vein and a nick was made in the skin with an 11 blade at the wire entrance site. The micropuncture wire and inner dilator were removed and a short angled Storq wire was advanced into the IVC using fluoroscopic guidance. We then advanced the included sheath into the IVC with the tip in the distal IVC.

Next we performed an IVC venogram showing a widely patent IVC and L renal vein. The sheath was advanced to the level of the left renal vein and the Denali IVC filter was placed and delivered such that the proximal hook was right at the level of the left renal vein.

A final shot confirmed placement of the IVC filter with no significant kinking of the filter noted.

The sheath was removed and manual compression was held over the access site before being dressed with gauze and a bandage. The patient was brought to the recovery room with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

4) Ultrasound guided R popliteal vein access
5) Selection of the right common iliac vein, then IVC
6) Placement of a 50cm infusion length EKOS thrombolysis catheter
7) Initiation of catheter directed thrombolysis

**SPECIMENS:** None

**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
The IVC was patent with no thrombus. The Renal vein was patent. The femoral and iliac veins were noted to have thrombus.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the right neck was prepped and draped in the usual fashion ultrasound evaluation of the R common femoral vein showed the vein to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos. 1% lidocaine plain was infiltrated at the planned site of vein access and a micropuncture needle was used to access the right common femoral vein using ultrasound guidance. The micro-puncture wire was advanced into the common femoral vein and a nick was made in the skin with an 11 blade at the wire entrance site. A final shot confirmed placement of the IVC filter with no significant kinking of the filter noted. The sheath was removed and manual compression was held over the access site before being dressed with gauze and a bandage.

We then flipped the patient into the prone position and prepped and draped the R posterior popliteal area. We then used 1% lidocaine to infiltrate the planned site of access. We used ultrasound guidance to access the popliteal vein with a micropuncture needle. We then advanced a micropuncture wire into the femoral vein followed by the sheath. We performed a femoral vein angiogram which showed a patent mid deep femoral vein with an occlusion at the proximal femoral vein. We placed a Storq wire into the femoral vein up to the area of stenosis and then upsized to an 11cm 6Fr sheath. Next we used a bernstein catheter and an angled glide wire to cross the venous occlusion of the common femoral vein on the right external iliac vein and the common femoral vein and we crossed the contralateral common iliac stent. We performed an IVC angiogram to confirm that we were intraluminal. Next we exchanged for a Storq wire and placed a 50cm EKOS infusion catheter. We confirmed its position initiated TPA infusion at 1 mg/hr via the infusion catheter and 500 units of Heparin an hour via the sheath side port. The patient was turned supine and brought to the ICU with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to ICU in stable condition

## Deep Vein Thrombosis - Suction Thrombectomy

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Acute LLE ilio-femoral DVT

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) Ultrasound guided R internal jugular vein access
2) IVC venogram
3) Placement of Denali Bard IVC filter
4) Ultrasound guided L femoral vein access
5) Selection of the left common iliac vein, then IVC
6) Left iliac vein angiogram
7) Initiation of suction thrombectomy with Penumbra Flash (16Fr)
8) Balloon angioplasty of the L common iliac vein with an 10mmx40mm Dorado balloon
9) Stenting of L common iliac vein with a 16mmx100mm self expanding stent
10) Post-angioplasty with a 12mmx40mm Dorado balloon

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local anesthesia with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 5 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
The IVC was patent with no thrombus. The Renal vein was patent. The femoral and iliac veins were noted to have thrombus. High-grade stenosis measuring at least 2 to 3 cm in length at the level of the iliac bifurcation, consistent with May-Thurner syndrome, involving the left proximal common iliac vein.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the right neck was prepped and draped in the usual fashion ultrasound evaluation of the R common femoral vein showed the vein to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos. 1% lidocaine plain was infiltrated at the planned site of vein access and a micropuncture needle was used to access the right common femoral vein using ultrasound guidance. The micro-puncture wire was advanced into the common femoral vein and a nick was made in the skin with an 11 blade at the wire entrance site. The micropuncture wire and inner dilator were removed and a short angled Storq wire was advanced into the IVC using fluoroscopic guidance. We then advanced the included sheath into the IVC with the tip in the distal IVC.

Next we performed an IVC venogram showing a widely patent IVC and L and R renal vein. The sheath was advanced to the level of the left renal vein and the Denali IVC filter was placed and delivered such that the proximal hook was right at the level of the left renal vein. A final shot confirmed placement of the IVC filter with no significant kinking of the filter noted. The sheath was removed and manual compression was held over the access site before being dressed with gauze and a bandage.

We then prepped and draped the groins as well as the left lower extremity. We evaluated the femoral vein in the mid-thigh with ultrasound, which was noted to compress with compression. We then infiltrated the planned site of access with 1% lidocaine and accessed the mid-thigh femoral vein with a micropuncture needle using ultrasound guidance. Next, we advanced our micropuncture wire followed by the micropuncture sheath. We then performed a femoral angiogram confirming that we were within the femoral vein. The femoral vein was free of thrombus however contrast got hung up at the level of the femoral head.

Next, we placed the exchange Storq wire, made a nick in the skin with an 11 blade, and upsized to a 6 French sheath. The wire was noted to get held up at the femoral head on the left. We then exchanged for a 10 French dilator, followed by a 12 French dilator and a 17 French Penumbra sheath. We performed a left femoral venogram again, showing the contrast being held up within the femoral vein and not going past the femoral head. We advanced our 16 French aspiration catheter up to the femoral head and ran the device per the instructions, performing suction thrombectomy and making multiple passes all the way up to the L5 region from the femoral head. We noted thrombus within the collection canister of the Penumbra device. A total length of 17 cm of thrombus was removed.

At this point, we performed another venogram showing resolution of thrombus from the external iliac vein on the left, as well as the femoral vein on the left, and the distal common iliac vein on the left, with contrast being hung up at the level of the iliac bifurcation. We needed an angle to glide the wire through a Bernstein catheter to cross this narrowing with an exchange for the exchange wire. We ballooned this region first with an 8 mm x 40 mm Dorado balloon followed by a 10 mm x 40 mm Dorado balloon. At this point, we were able to cross the lesion without any problem. We performed another venogram confirming that we were within the inferior vena cava and also defining the length of the narrowing.

Specifically, our venogram revealed high-grade stenosis measuring at least 2 to 3 cm in length at the level of the iliac bifurcation, consistent with May-Thurner syndrome, involving the left proximal common iliac vein. In addition, there was narrowing and scarring of the common iliac vein up to the iliac bifurcation. The external iliac artery was clear of thrombus and did not appear scarred or diseased. Next, we deployed a 16 mm x 100 mm Medtronic self-expanding venous stent. We post-angioplastied this with a 12 mm x 40 mm Dorado balloon. The completion angiogram showed excellent flow through the previously stenotic and narrowed left common iliac vein. At this point, we removed our sheath and wire and held manual compression for 10 minutes, then closed the skin incision with interrupted Monocryl.

The patient tolerated the procedure well and was brought to the recovery room with stable vital signs.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition
