## AV Fistula Creation

### Sample Report 1 — Radiocephalic (Forearm)

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Chronic Renal Insufficiency

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
Left forearm radio-cephalic arterio-venous fistula creation

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local + sedation + arm block

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 15 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
The AV fistula had a great thrill and low resistant flow on doppler evaluation.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After sedation was administered, the left upper extremity was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. We mapped out the cephalic vein at the wrist and the radial artery with ultrasound. The vein was noted to be fully compressible with no intrinsic echos. The vein and artery were several centimeters apart and because of this we chose to make a longitudinal incision over the vein and the artery and tunnel the vein. The patient had received a left upper extremity block which worked well when tested. We started by dissecting out the cephalic vein and its branches in the distal forearm (10cm above wrist) and controlled all the cephalic veins branches. Next we dissected out the radial artery in the same area. We ensured that we had enough vein length to tunnel and reach the radial artery. Of note the radial artery was noted to be soft and have a palpable pulse. Next we administered 5,000 units of heparin and after 3 minutes clamped the radial artery proximally and distally with a bulldog. Next we transected the distal end of the vein and ligated the distal end with 3-0 silk sutures. All the side branches were doubly ligated with 3-0 silk sutures. Next we tunneled the vein using a Kelly clamp from the venous incision site to the arterial incision medially. We made sure there were no areas of kinking. The vein had been marked and dilated with heparinized saline to ensure no twisting. Next we made a longitudinal arteriotomy measuring 3mm in the artery and beveled the end of the tunneled cephalic vein. An end to side anastomosis was created using 6-0 Prolene suture with BV-1 needle in a running fashion. We flushed the anastomosis prior to completing the anastomosis and completed the anastomosis and noted there to be a thrill in the vein. Next we obtained hemostasis and closed the incisions in two layers. First we used a 3-0 Vicryl in the subcutaneous layer then a 4-0 monocryl in the dermal layer. We used glue for the skin.

The patient tolerated the procedure well and returned to the recovery room with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient discharged same day with fistula care instructions

### Sample Report 2 — Radiocephalic (Alternative)

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
ESRD with need for long term dialysis

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
LUE AV fistula creation

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Arm block

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 25 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
Nice thrill in outflow vein at the end of the procedure.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After a left arm block by anesthesia and the administration of sedation we evaluated the cephalic vein in the left forearm which was noted to be around 3mm in diameter. Next we made a longitudinal incision over the distal cephalic vein and used electrocautery and Metzenbaum scissors to dissect and mobilize the cephalic vein. All the side branches were ligated with 3-0 silk sutures.

Next we made a longitudinal incision over the slightly more proximal radial artery and used electrocautery and metzenbaum scissors to dissect down and expose the radial artery. This was controlled circumferentially with vessel loops.

The cephalic vein was marked and transected distally with scissors and we ligated the distal end with a 3-0 silk suture. We dilated the vein with an angiocath and heparinized saline.

We administered 5,000 units of heparin and clamped the radial artery with bulldog clamps. We then made a longitudinal incision with an 11 blade and extended this with Potts scissors. Next we beveled the end of the cephalic vein and made an end to side anastomosis with a running 6-0 prolene suture. Prior to completing the anastomosis we flushed the artery both antegrade and retrograde and then completed the anastomosis and tied down our knot. The cephalic outflow vein had a nice thrill. We ensured that the vein was not kinked and then after obtaining hemostasis closed the incisions in two layers, first a 3-0 vicryl layer then a 4-0 Monocryl in the skin.

The patient was brought to the recovery room with vital signs stable.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient discharged same day with fistula care instructions

## Tunneled Dialysis Catheter Placement

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Acute renal failure

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
Exchange of temporary dialysis catheter and placement of a Left internal jugular tunneled dialysis catheter (23 cm Bard)

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** Local anesthesia with sedation

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 5 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
Both ports withdrew blood easily. Tip of the catheter was in the right atrium. She pulled out her temporary dialysis catheter out of her right groin earlier today.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the right neck was prepped and draped in the usual fashion 1% lidocaine plain was infiltrated at the planned site of catheter tunneling and around the current indwelling temporary dialysis catheter. A short stiff Amplatz wire was placed into the indwelling catheter under fluoroscopic guidance. The tip was noted to be in the inferior vena cava. The old catheter was removed and the larger of the included dilators was placed to plug the tract. A nick was made in the skin with an 11 blade at the wire entrance site and at the planned tunneling catheter entrance site. We then infiltrated the tunneling tract from two fingerbreadths below the mid-clavicular line to the neck wire access site in a curvilinear fashion.

The planned tunneling tract was dilated with a mosquito clamp. Next we tunneled the catheter with the included tunneling device from the chest incision to the neck incision.

Next the included two dilators were used to dilate the tract into the internal jugular vein using fluoroscopic guidance up to the peel-away sheath. The inner dilator and wire were removed and the tip of the tunneled catheter was advanced into the SVC up to the right atrium using fluoroscopic guidance. The catheter was not kinked. Both ports withdrew blood easily and were flushed first with normal saline followed by 1,000 units/ml of Heparin. Both ports were capped. A biopatch was placed around the catheter exit site and the catheter was secured with two interrupted 3-0 nylon sutures. The neck incision was closed with two interrupted 3-0 monocryl sutures and the sites were dressed with gauze and tegaderm.

The patient tolerated the procedure well and returned to the recovery room with vital signs stable.

This tunneled dialysis catheter is ok to be used for dialysis.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition
