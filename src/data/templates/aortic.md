## EVAR (Endovascular Aneurysm Repair)

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Asymptomatic Infra-renal Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
1) ultrasound guided access of bilateral common femoral arteries
2) bilateral common femoral artery fem-check angiogram
3) bilateral common femoral artery preclose Perclose placement x 2 in each CFA
4) Abdominal aortic aneurysm repair with:
   - 28mmx14mmx12cm main body Gore Excluder
   - L 14mm x 12cm iliac limb extension Gore iliac extension
   - R 14mm x 14mm iliac limb extension Gore iliac extension
5) post balloon angioplasty with Gore aortic balloon
6) completion aorto-iliac angiogram
7) closure of bilateral common femoral arteries with Perclose devices

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** Dr. Esther Bae
**ANESTHESIA:** GETA

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 50 mL
**FLUIDS:** Contrast: 103ml, Urine output: 150ml
**SPECIMENS:** none
**DRAINS:** none
**COMPLICATIONS:** none
**FLUORO:** 9min

**FINDINGS:**
Patent bilateral renal arteries with patent hypogastric arteries bilaterally with type II endoleaks noted.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of general anesthesia, the patient's groins were prepped and draped in the usual fashion. An ultrasound evaluation of bilateral femoral arteries revealed some mild calcification anteriorly, especially on the left. We next accessed bilateral common femoral arteries using ultrasound guidance, utilizing a micropuncture needle followed by advancement of a micropuncture wire and the micropuncture sheath. We then performed fem-check angiograms bilaterally confirming that we were within the common femoral arteries. Next, we placed our Storq wires and 8 French sheaths. We then deployed 2 Perclose devices in each groin, which were deployed in the Perclose technique so we did not cinch down the knots; we secured the sutures of the 2 Perclose devices per groin. Next we administered 8,000 units of heparin. Then, we placed our 8 French sheaths and exchanged for stiff and Amplatz wires, followed by serial dilation starting with a 12 French dilation from a Gore Excluder. We then placed an 18 French Gore DrySeal on the left and a 1 French Gore DrySeal on the right.

Next, we opened up a 28 millimeter by 14 millimeter by 12 centimeter Gore main body Excluder and positioned it at the level of L1 and L2. We placed a pigtail through our 12 French sheath and performed an aortoiliac angiogram, which showed patent renal arteries, the abdominal aortic aneurysm sac, and iliac arteries. We then positioned our endograft just above the renal arteries, the left renal artery being the lowest. Next, we deployed the endograft and performed two further angiograms to confirm that the renal arteries were patent. We then pulled back our 12 French sheath and used a Bernstein catheter to select for the contralateral limb. With an angled Glide wire, we confirmed that we were within the devices using an Omni flush which was able to be twirled without difficulty. We then replaced the stiff wire and performed a retrograde sheath angiogram on the right and placed a 14 millimeter by 14 cm Gore iliac limb extension on that side, such that it extended almost up to the hypogastric artery on the right. This was deployed, and we then finished deploying the limb on the left, removed the device delivery system, and placed our marker catheter to perform a retrograde iliac angiogram on the left, measuring out the distance and placing a 14 millimeter by 10 centimeter Gore iliac limb extension on the left. This was deployed to extend up to the left hypogastric artery.

Next, we ballooned the top part of the aorta as well as bilateral limbs with a Gore balloon. We performed a kissing ballooning technique at the bifurcation due to the narrow distal abdominal aorta. Next, we performed a completion final aorto-iliac angiogram which showed patent renal arteries, an excluded abdominal aortic aneurysm with a late type 2 endoleak in the sac noted by filling of some lumbar arteries, followed by patent iliac arteries and patent hypogastric and external iliac arteries bilaterally. We then removed our sheaths serially and cinched down the Perclose devices on each side, which worked without difficulty. We were able to obtain hemostasis without problems.

We then closed the skin wounds with interrupted 4-0 Monocryl sutures, placed Dermabond and dressings, and the patient was extubated and brought to the recovery room with stable vital signs and palpable femoral pulses distal to the closure devices.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition
