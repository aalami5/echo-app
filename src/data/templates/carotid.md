## Carotid Endarterectomy (CEA)

### Sample Report 1

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Symptomatic R ICA Stenosis

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Same

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
Right Carotid Endarterectomy With Patch Angioplasty Repair

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** Anna Chun, PA
**ANESTHESIA:** General

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** 2 mL
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** None
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** None

**FINDINGS:**
Posterior ulcerated plaque removed and nice ICA doppler sounds at end of case and patient moved all 4 extremities after the procedure.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
After the administration of general anesthesia, the patient's right neck was prepped and draped in the usual fashion. A curvilinear incision was made with a 15-blade over the sternocleidomastoid muscle. Electrocautery was used to dissect down through the platysma, exposing the sternocleidomastoid muscle. This muscle was reflected laterally, exposing the internal jugular vein. We then switched to Metzenbaum scissors and mobilized the internal jugular vein. Notably, there were 2 branches that were doubly ligated with 3-0 silk sutures. Subsequently, we mobilized the internal jugular vein laterally, exposing the underlying common carotid artery. Two ansa cervicalis nerves were observed crossing the common carotid artery; the first nerve was mobilized laterally, while the second was transected and ligated with 3-0 silk sutures. We then gained control of the common carotid artery with a doubly looped vessel loop after mobilizing it, and gained control of the internal carotid artery distally as well as the external carotid artery proximally. All vessels were controlled with doubly looped vessel loops. Additionally, the superior thyroid artery was also controlled with a 3-0 silk suture doubly looped.

Next, we administered 7000 units of heparin. The vagus nerve and hypoglossal nerve were both identified and preserved. We then clamped the common carotid artery and the internal carotid artery, using vessel loops to gain control of the external carotid artery. We made an incision with an 11-blade anteriorly to create our longitudinal arteriotomy, extending it proximally and distally with pots scissors. We then placed an Inari shunt; first, the internal carotid artery end was placed, followed by the common carotid artery end, and we confirmed flow through the shunt using Doppler ultrasound.

Next, we performed an endarterectomy on the plaque within the carotid bifurcation using Penfield dissectors and pots scissors. After completing the endarterectomy, we tacked down the distal end of the intima with 3-0 Prolene sutures placed individually. We then irrigated copiously, and once satisfied that there was no further loose debris, we sutured on our bovine pericardial patch with 5-0 Prolene sutures, starting with the distal end. Before completing the patch angioplasty repair, we removed the shunt and flushed the external carotid artery first, followed by the internal carotid artery, and then the common carotid artery. We then flushed the repair with heparinized saline, completed the repair, tightened the sutures, and opened up the common carotid artery to the external carotid artery, followed by the internal carotid artery.

We reversed the heparin with 30 mg of protamine. The wound was closed in two layers: first, the platysma layer with 3-0 Vicryl, followed by the skin with 4-0 Monocryl. The patient was extubated and was moving all extremities, responsive, and stable in the recovery room.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition

### Sample Report 2

**PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Symptomatic left internal carotid artery stenosis, 80%

**POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS:**
Symptomatic left internal carotid artery stenosis, 80%

**PROCEDURE PERFORMED:**
Left carotid endarterectomy with bovine pericardial patch angioplasty

**SURGEON:** Oliver Aalami, MD
**ASSISTANT:** None
**ANESTHESIA:** General endotracheal anesthesia

**ESTIMATED BLOOD LOSS:** TBD
**FLUIDS:** None
**SPECIMENS:** Atherosclerotic plaque, sent to pathology
**DRAINS:** None
**COMPLICATIONS:** None
**URINE OUTPUT:** No Foley

**FINDINGS:**
Severe atherosclerotic plaque involving the left internal carotid artery with approximately 80% stenosis. The plaque extended from the distal common carotid artery across the bifurcation into the proximal internal carotid artery.

**INDICATIONS:**
The patient presented with symptomatic left internal carotid artery stenosis of 80%. Given the symptomatic nature of the disease, the patient met criteria for carotid endarterectomy to reduce future stroke risk.

**DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE:**
The patient was brought to the operating room and placed in the supine position. After induction of general endotracheal anesthesia, the neck was prepped and draped in the usual sterile fashion. A timeout was performed confirming correct patient, procedure, and laterality.

An oblique incision was made along the anterior border of the left sternocleidomastoid muscle. Dissection was carried through the platysma. The common carotid artery, internal carotid artery, and external carotid artery were identified and circumferentially dissected. Vessel loops were placed around each vessel for control.

The patient was systemically heparinized. After achieving adequate ACT, vascular clamps were applied to the common carotid artery, internal carotid artery, and external carotid artery. An arteriotomy was made in the common carotid artery and extended across the bifurcation into the internal carotid artery. A Sundt intraluminal shunt was placed to maintain cerebral perfusion during the endarterectomy.

The atherosclerotic plaque was identified and a careful endarterectomy was performed, developing the appropriate plane and feathering the distal endpoint. The plaque was removed in its entirety and sent to pathology.

The Sundt shunt was removed. A bovine pericardial patch was fashioned and the arteriotomy was closed using a running 6-0 Prolene suture. Prior to completing the closure, the vessels were back-bled and forward-flushed to remove any debris. Flow was restored first to the external carotid artery, then to the internal carotid artery.

Hemostasis was confirmed. The wound was irrigated. The platysma was reapproximated and the skin was closed in layers. A sterile dressing was applied. The patient was awakened from anesthesia, was neurologically intact, and was transferred to the recovery area in stable condition.

**DISPOSITION:**
Patient transferred to recovery in stable condition
